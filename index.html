Model and assumptions

Pdyn [nPa] = 1.6726×10⁻⁶ np [cm⁻³] V [km/s]²

Protons only; dynamic pressure is ρV² with no ½ factor. Helium and thermal pressure are omitted.

Magnetopause: Shue et al. (1998): r = r₀[2/(1+cos θ)]α; r₀ = [10.22+1.29 tanh(0.184(Bz+8.14))]Pdyn−1/6.6 RE; α = (0.58−0.007Bz)(1+0.024 ln Pdyn). Bz is in nT; pressure is in nPa.

Bow shock: Jelínek et al. (2012): ρ² = 4rBS,0(rBS,0−x)/1.17², with rBS,0 = 15.02Pdyn−1/6.55 RE and ρ²=y²+z². All coordinates are in RE.

Surface field: Both surfaces are scaled by (BE/30,000 nT)1/3 from dipole pressure balance. This is an educational extrapolation, not part of either empirical fit; BE does not enter either auroral model.

Solar-wind aurora: CH-Aurora-2014 (Xiong & Lühr, 2014) uses the Newell coupling function Em = V4/3(By²+Bz²)1/3sin8/3(θ/2)/1000, θ = atan2(|By|, Bz), with V in km/s, B in nT and Em in the paper's scaled mV/m convention. Each hemisphere and boundary has five ellipse parameters fitted as p₂Em²+p₁Em+p₀ (Tables 2–3); MLT is evaluated along each ellipse. Slider values represent solar wind sustained for at least the paper's 3 h memory window, so its 30 min equatorward and 15 min poleward delays give the same steady Em. No CHAMP observation adjustment is applied. The 2000–2010 fit is an empirical FAC-boundary estimate, not an auroral observation or a real-time forecast.

Kp aurora: Starkov coefficients as presented by Sigernes et al. (2011), with Kp→AL and MLT. Southern Kp boundaries are mirrored from the Northern fit. Kp is independent of solar-wind sliders; 0–6 is shown because high-Kp fits deteriorate.

References: Xiong & Lühr (2014), CH-Aurora-2014; Newell et al. (2007), coupling function; Shue et al. (1998); Jelínek et al. (2012); Sigernes et al. (2011), Starkov coefficients. Jelínek et al. recommend their bow-shock fit for dayside low latitudes and 0.6–11 nPa.
